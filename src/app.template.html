Also avoid

The solver picks foods greedily by how much of the remaining gap a serving of each closes, weighting nutrients by how few foods supply them at all — so vitamin B12, iodine and choline pull hard. It then tunes portions until every target is met or it runs out of room, keeping portions to about two servings of any food and at most two meat or fish mains a day. It respects upper limits, then brings calories to your target. A complete day usually takes 9–13 foods.

Where nutrients actually come from

Targets

The US Dietary Reference Intakes for your sex and age: the RDA where one exists, otherwise the Adequate Intake. EPA+DHA (no DRI) and molybdenum (USDA doesn’t measure it, so most foods read zero) are shown for information only.

Upper limits

Kept for preformed vitamin A, selenium, zinc, calcium, iron, sodium and others; plant carotenoids don’t count toward the vitamin A limit. Manganese’s 11 mg limit is shown but not enforced: whole grains, nuts and legumes routinely pass it without harm.

Food values

USDA FoodData Central for the stated preparation, with newer Foundation Foods lab values where they exist, and iodine from the USDA/FDA/ODS Iodine Database. A value that isn’t known is zero, never guessed, so a day that meets every target has margin. Real food varies by a few percent or more: treat 100% as a soft floor.

Processing levels

NOVA groups (Monteiro et al.), applied to each food as typically sold in a US supermarket; a bakery loaf is group 3 even where packaged ones would be 4. Ultra-processed foods (group 4) are left out, except two fortified plant milks kept as a vegan’s food source of B12. Added vitamins alone don’t make a food group 4.

Fortified foods

Fortified plant milks and juices, iodized salt, and enriched bread and pasta are marked, and use the Nutrition Facts label of one widely sold US product each, so check your own carton. To plan without them, pick Fortified foods under “Also avoid” in Auto-solve.

Sodium

A ceiling, never a gap: the bar turns amber above 2,300 mg, the level the 2019 DRI review ties to chronic-disease risk (the AI is 1,500 mg). Salt at the table isn’t counted unless you add the iodized-salt entry.

Vitamin K and iodine

Vitamin K counts K1 and K2 together, since both serve the same enzyme. Iodine in seaweed varies several-fold (the nori figure is for toasted sheets), and kelp is far higher and genuinely risky.

Diet patterns

Food-selection rules, not certifications; “raw” means edible as listed without cooking. Some patterns can’t meet every target (carnivore has no fiber or vitamin C; vegans need a fortified food for B12), and the app says so.

Not medical advice

This is arithmetic, not a diet prescription. Talk to a clinician or dietitian before big changes, especially if you’re pregnant, managing a condition, or on medication (vitamin K and warfarin, for example).
